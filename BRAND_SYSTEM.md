## 2. Master Persona — Institution Builder

### Behavioral definition (per TK's own sharpening)
**The Institution Builder: calm, unhurried, certain — the kind of authority that doesn't need to announce itself.**

> Composure is not coldness. The brand has feeling — especially when it speaks to founders.

### What an Institution Builder does — the verb bank

| Use these verbs | Avoid these |
|-----------------|-------------|
| codifies, sequences, diagnoses, governs, sustains, hands over, anchors, stewards, integrates, retains | hustles, pitches, scales, disrupts, hacks, accelerates, blitzes, crushes, dominates, scoops |

### Audience-specific expressions

| Layer | Persona | Speaks to | Function |
|-------|---------|-----------|----------|
| Master | **Institution Builder** | Market, partners, founders, CXOs | Sets larger purpose and category-defining ambition |
| Founder/CXO | **Boardroom Architect** | Promoters, family businesses, CEOs, corp dev heads | Makes purpose personal: legacy, succession, professionalisation, scale |
| Execution | **Integration Leadership Partner** | L2/L3 leaders, integration teams, functional heads | Converts vision into operating discipline: milestones, governance, value capture |

Both rooms hear: **composure first, conviction second, evidence third.**

### Two rooms, one brand

> **Founder hears:** "You built a company. Now build an institution."
> **Integration lead hears:** "The deal is approved in the boardroom. Value is created through integration leadership."
> Same brand. The first names the *why*. The second names the *how*.

## 4. Market Thesis (the larger frame)

> The mid-market arena — not merely mid-market companies — sits at the centre of the opportunity.

> As India moves toward 2047, the mid-market arena will become one of the most important theatres of inorganic growth. Large companies will acquire capability-rich firms. Mid-market businesses will consolidate fragmented sectors. Smaller firms will scale through combination. Founder-led companies will professionalise into enduring institutions. The M&A Leadership Platform exists to build the boardroom judgment, integration leadership, and enterprise-wide capability required for this next generation of acquirers.

Five buyer archetypes the brand serves:
1. **Large companies** — acquiring mid-market firms for capability, geography, sector consolidation
2. **Mid-market consolidators** — scaling through smaller acquisitions
3. **Small firms scaling up** — using M&A to graduate into mid-market
4. **PE-backed platforms** — consolidating fragmented sectors, professionalising operating models
5. **Founder-led / family businesses** — succession, professionalisation, institutional handover

## 6. Voice Rules (use this table when writing)

| Use more | Use less | Avoid |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Conviction | Training program | War room |
| Integration leadership | Course | Play room |
| Institutional scale | Modules | Deal frenzy |
| Boardroom judgment | Templates | Salesy jargon |
| Capability | Features | Overclaiming |
| Value capture | Networking opportunity | Startup-style SaaS hype |
| Milestone discipline | Certificate | Generic consulting brochure tone |
| Founder legacy | Generic marketplace | Brochure-register prose |

> **Rows are independent — read each column top to bottom.**

### Terminology decisions
- **"Integration Leadership Room"** replaces "war room" everywhere. The capability is *integration leadership*. The operating place where it happens is the *Integration Leadership Room*.
- We don't have "courses." We have **cohorts** and **leadership forums**.
- We don't have "modules." We have **layers** (L1/L2/L3).
- We don't have "users" of the platform. We have **member firms** and **leaders**.

## 7. Brand Animal — Asiatic Lion

### Why this animal (locked in — TK's five reasons)

1. **Pride structure mirrors L1/L2/L3.** The Asiatic Lion is the only big cat with a social structure. The pride mirrors the platform's three-layer architecture: dominant individuals set strategic direction (L1), mid-range members execute the hunt (L2), the wider pride sustains and protects the gain (L3). No other animal in Indian fauna gives that organizational metaphor built in.

2. **Composed, not frantic.** Lions don't chase. They wait, assess, and move with overwhelming certainty when the moment is right. *That is the behavioral definition of "boardroom-grade."* The opposite of deal frenzy.

3. **Rare and India-specific.** The Asiatic Lion exists nowhere else on earth except the Gir Forest. Geographic exclusivity mirrors the platform's positioning — India's first, India's only. It is not the Bengal Tiger (national animal, overused in Indian branding) or the generic savanna lion of global brands. Specifically, irreducibly Indian.

4. **The Ashoka connection — governance over conquest.** The Lion Capital of Ashoka (India's national emblem) features four Asiatic Lions on a single pillar. Erected not as a symbol of conquest but as a marker of institutional intent — *the moment an emperor chose governance over war, legacy over transaction.* The platform's core promise is institution-building. The Ashoka Lion is, historically, the symbol of exactly that turn.

5. **Stillness over the roar.** The power lies in stillness, not aggression. A lion at rest, watching — not a lion mid-roar. The latter is every generic brand that wants to look strong. The former is an institution that doesn't need to.

### How it appears in brand expression

> The recurring image is a lion at rest, watching.
>
> Never roaring. Never mid-charge. Never aggressive.

Power in stillness: composed authority, the silence before the move, social intelligence, patience.

### Pride composition (for L1/L2/L3 visuals)
- **L1** = pride elders deciding — a small group at rest, surveying
- **L2** = mid-pride executing — coordinated, alert
- **L3** = the wider pride sustaining — distributed, patient

## 8. Image Asset Spec

This is the rule set for any image we generate or accept for the brand. Used by Track A2 (lion regeneration) and Track B (full visual system).

### Composition
- Subject is **still or seated**. Eyes alert. Head up.
- **No** bared teeth, mid-roar, mid-stride, attack pose.
- Setting is **council / stage / chamber** — not savanna / hunt / battlefield.
- For multi-lion compositions, the **pride is at rest together**, not in motion.

### Color & material
- Palette: deep navy (currently `#0a1628`) + faceted gold (current asset family).
- No pure black, no pure gray. Tinted neutrals only.
- Geometry: **low-poly faceted** *or* **composed photographic**. Never photorealistic-aggressive.

### Atmosphere
- Constellation/lattice background suggesting strategy, network, intelligence.
- Theatrical lighting — single soft key, restrained shadow.
- Negative space is the brand's calm. Don't over-fill.

### Reject if
- Roar, charge, bared teeth, mid-stride.
- Lone-savanna or hunt-scene composition.
- Pure black or saturated jewel-tone palettes.
- Photorealistic close-up that reads "stock photo."
- Generic-strong-brand cliché (lion in front of flag, lion on rocky outcrop snarling, etc.).

This spec applies to every image touchpoint until Track B's `design.md` ratifies or amends it.

## 10. What the Brand Is NOT

(Constraint list — kill anything that drifts here.)

- A training company
- A course platform
- **Not "a SaaS deal-room tool" — we are guided M&A capability infrastructure. Software is part of it; the brand isn't.**
- A conference circuit
- A "marketplace" for buyers and sellers
- A consulting retainer in disguise
- A networking club
- A startup-style growth hacky platform
- **A brochure.** v5 read as a brochure. v6 must read as an institution speaking with composure and feeling.
