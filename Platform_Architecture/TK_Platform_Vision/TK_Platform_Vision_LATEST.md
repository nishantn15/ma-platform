# TK Platform Architecture Vision - v1

**Source:** WhatsApp from TK, 2026-07-03 05:56 (verbatim).
**Status:** TK's raw thinking, to be integrated into a holistic platform plan (do not treat as final spec).
**Context:** TK is iterating course content with the Prof in parallel; this note opens the second track - the platform/product architecture that wraps the learning experience into a 5-year capability-building workspace.

Hi Nishant,

As we are now going deeper into the course architecture — module themes by level, content, pedagogical method, learning flow, exercises and annual journey design — I am meeting Prof again this afternoon to iterate further.

In parallel, I thought we should also start thinking through the broader platform architecture, especially the physical/digital platform layer that will sit around the learning experience. My sense is that the M&A Learning Platform should not only support course delivery, but also become a longer-term capability-building ecosystem for participating companies.

At this stage, I am imagining the platform around the following modules and features:

1. Learning Onboarding and Participant Journey

This would support the full course experience for individual participants and company accounts.

Possible features:

* Course onboarding and participant orientation
* Module-wise course deliverables
* Exercises, assignments and answer submissions
* Public polls, group reflections and live classroom inputs
* Personal takeaways: lessons for self, notes for self, leadership reflections
* Individual learning journey: course completion, next stage, next steps
* Participant dashboard: progress, submissions, commitments and development plan
* Company-level dashboard: who is at what stage, completion status, cohort progress, pending actions and engagement levels

The idea is to make the learning journey visible, trackable and cumulative across the year.

2. Ideas Funnel

This module would capture ideas generated during the course and help companies nurture them over time.

Possible stages:

* Idea submitted
* Brainstormed
* Evaluated
* Shortlisted
* Sponsored
* Funded
* Team assembled
* Pilot kicked off
* Milestones tracked
* Scaled / parked / closed

The important point is that ideas should not die after the workshop. The platform should help companies track them over a longer horizon — potentially up to five years — because meaningful innovation and capability-building often need time to mature.

3. Patent-to-Enterprise Exchange

This would be a dedicated module to support the Patent-to-Enterprise Exchange.

Possible features:

* Patent / technology repository
* Institution, department and inventor profiles
* Patent briefs translated into business language
* Industry use cases and application areas
* Expression of interest from companies
* Collaboration pathways: licensing, sponsored research, joint development, pilot, startup creation, investment or acquisition-adjacent opportunity
* Follow-up tracking with institute, inventor and company teams
* Milestone tracking over a longer cycle

This can begin with IIT Roorkee and later include selected patents and technologies from other institutions as coverage expands.

 4. Target Sourcing

This module would help companies think through acquisition-led capability building.

Possible features:

* Capability gap definition
* Target search criteria
* Sector / capability / geography filters
* Longlist and shortlist tracking
* Target profiles
* Strategic fit assessment
* Build-buy-partner-acquire comparison
* Initial diligence notes
* Internal decision tracking
* Relationship and outreach tracking

The purpose is not just target listing, but capability-led target sourcing.

 5. Deal Integration Module

This module would support the post-acquisition integration journey.

Possible features:

* Deal lifecycle tracking
* Deal thesis and integration thesis
* Day 1 readiness
* 30/60/90-day plan
* Workstream charters
* Functional integration milestones
* Synergy tracking
* Risk, issue and dependency tracker
* Leadership and communication plan
* Capability transfer plan
* Integration governance dashboard
* Long-term value realization tracking

For integration, I am thinking the platform should support a five-year window wherever relevant. Many acquisitions do not become truly successful in the first 100 days or even the first year. Capability transfer, cultural absorption, value realization and institutionalization often take much longer.

Five-Year Access

One thought I wanted to discuss with you: for modules such as Ideas Funnel, Patent-to-Enterprise Exchange and Deal Integration, we may design the platform around a five-year usage horizon for each onboarded account.

Even if the client does not renew the annual learning/capability development program the following year, they could continue using these platform modules for five years from onboarding.

I had initially thought of offering lifetime access, but I feel that may reduce perceived value. A five-year window feels more intentional and defensible. It aligns with the reality that:

* serious ideas need time to mature
* patents need time to move toward commercialization
* acquisition integration and capability transfer can take several years
* enterprise capability building is not a one-year exercise

So the proposition becomes:

We are not merely offering a course. We are giving participating companies a five-year capability-building workspace to traprioritizepatents, acquisition opportunities and integration journeys.

The broader ambition is to develop this as an ecosystem for leaders, peer learning, idea-to-industrialization pathways, patent-to-enterprise collaboration, and acquisition-to-integration capability building. So our positioning becomes: The platform should not end when the classroom ends. It should become the company’s five-year workspace for converting learning into ideas, patents, acquisitions and integration outcomes.

Hope this makes sense. Would be good to brainstorm how we should shape the first version of the platform architecture and what features we should prioritize for MVP versus later phases.
