Phase 01 · Strategic Intent

Begin with the thesis. Not the deal.

Before a target is named, the board names the gap.

What the institution does

The Room opens with thesis work. Anchor's growth runway in BFSI is bound by specialty engineering capacity, not by client demand. The board commits the gap in writing: a five-year capability shortfall, with named consequences if it isn't closed. The thesis is the first instrument; everything downstream is a test of it.

The cohort's role: a closed peer forum stress-tests the thesis. Two CXOs from non-competing sectors challenge the assumptions. The platform documents the dissent. Strong theses survive contact with peers; brittle ones don't make it past Phase 01.

  • Capability gap statement, board-approved
  • Five-year scenario with do-nothing baseline
  • Acquisition-vs-build comparison, signed by Strategy Head
  • Peer forum dissent recorded against the thesis

Phase 02 · Target Selection

Map the field. Then commit to two.

A serious shortlist always lists why each name was rejected.

What the institution does

The platform's sourcing intelligence tracks 47 specialty data engineering firms in India between Rs 200 Cr and Rs 800 Cr. The Room narrows them to two by applying Anchor's thesis as a filter: founder-led, balance-sheet vulnerable, BFSI-friendly, senior bench depth. Polaris and an unnamed alternative make the cut.

The discipline: the rejected 45 are documented with reasons. Future revisits start from this map, not from scratch. The institution refuses to lose its own work.

  • 47 companies scanned, 45 rejected with named reasons
  • Two finalists, each with a thesis-fit memo
  • Sector-cohort peer review on selection rationale

Phase 03 · Deal Readiness

Prepare before diligence tests you.

Most acquirers are tested by diligence. Members arrive ready for it.

What the institution does

Eight readiness gates before approach. Anchor has its capability thesis, its integration thesis, its retention model for Polaris's senior bench, and its announcement narrative draft. Advisors are scoped, not retained yet. The CFO has run the price-discipline scenario through the board's risk envelope.

What changes: when Anchor calls Polaris's founder, the call is shorter and clearer than it would have been. Readiness compresses time.

  • Integration thesis drafted and aligned to capability thesis
  • Retention design for Polaris top 24 engineers
  • Board-approved walk-away price
  • Advisor RFP scope sent to three firms

Phase 04 · Diligence Guidance

The questions you forgot to ask.

Advisors run diligence. The Room asks where advisors don't look.

What the institution does

Standard diligence catches the standard risks. The Room layers an institution- specific prompt set: founder dependence on three named clients, attrition signal in the senior architecture bench, undisclosed pricing concessions in the Q4 renewal book, the cultural translation gap between Polaris's product stance and Anchor's services contract model.

What it surfaces here: two prompts hit. The pricing concessions were real and material; price is renegotiated by Rs 80 Cr.

Phase 05 · Integration Planning

Day 1, Day 30, Day 100. In writing.

The thesis becomes a calendar. Or it doesn't survive.

What the institution does

Twelve workstreams. Each with a charter, decision rights, escalation rules, and a Day 1 readiness scorecard. The IMO (Integration Management Office) is standing before close. The cadence is set: weekly steering for Day 1 to Day 30, fortnightly through Day 100, monthly to Year 1.

The non-obvious move: the platform's Integration Planning template is borrowed and adapted, not invented from scratch. Anchor saves eight weeks. Eight weeks is one quarter of synergy.

  • Twelve workstream charters, signed
  • Day 1 / Day 30 / Day 100 milestone map
  • IMO Director appointed, two months pre-close
  • Decision rights matrix, escalation paths documented

Phase 06 · Integration Milestones

The hundred days the deal pays for.

Synergy isn't the slide deck. Synergy is the Friday review.

What the institution does

The Friday review runs for ninety minutes, every week of the first hundred days. Synergy capture against plan, value leakage flags, retention pulse, governance decisions. The platform tracks it. The institution holds itself to it. The cohort sees the redacted dashboard and asks the questions Anchor's own people are too close to ask.

Where it earns the brand: when Polaris's biggest BFSI client issues a stop-work in week six, the response runtime is 36 hours. In a less prepared firm it would have been three weeks.

  • Weekly synergy capture against thesis
  • Retention pulse: top 24 named, weekly
  • Value-leakage register, gold-flagged
  • Cohort review: redacted dashboard, every fortnight

Phase 07 · Capability Building

The deal becomes a discipline.

A first acquisition is a project. A repeatable acquirer is an institution.

What the institution does

Year 1 closes. Anchor has its specialty data practice. The Room does the harder work: it converts the deal into an asset that compounds. A postmortem captures what worked. The integration playbook is updated with three new entries. The L2 Execution cohort gets the redacted case. Two senior engineers from Polaris graduate from L3 Champions to L2 Execution Leaders.

What's different by Year 2: when Anchor evaluates a second acquisition, the thesis work is faster, the diligence prompt set is sharper, the IMO is a known operating mode rather than a one-off. The next deal pays for itself faster than this one did.

  • Postmortem: 14 entries · 3 promoted to standard playbook
  • Cohort case study, redacted, shared in L2
  • Two engineers move from L3 Champions to L2 Execution
